## Outcome

Answer whether there is evidence that further improvement is impractical for
this pinned model, single RTX 5090, single-session workload and accepted
quality envelope. Distinguish that bounded engineering conclusion from the
unprovable claim that no implementation can possibly be faster. Exhausting a
kernel-tuning ladder does not establish either a bandwidth bound or global
optimality. Read [OPT-115](OPT-115.md) and [OPT-116](OPT-116.md).

## Investigation

1. Recompute compulsory byte/compute and serial-dependency bounds from the
   final OPT-123 representation and measured access patterns. Produce separate
   bounds for original encodings and admitted lower-precision alternatives,
   and for prefill versus each decode context. Document reuse assumptions,
   measured bandwidth uncertainty, unpack/scale costs and unclassified time.
   A bound that excludes known required work is optimistic, not attainable
   performance; a roofline bottleneck is not proof of an optimal algorithm.
2. Locate post113, final Quartz and pinned llama on latency/quality/memory
   comparisons with matched request boundaries, generated token counts,
   capacities, graph/flash-attention settings, clocks and hardware occupancy.
   Report distance to modeled bounds and explained/unexplained residuals.
   A smaller allocated context or skipped host sampling is not an inference
   improvement under identical constraints.
3. Check that pinned llama represents the intended strong baseline: actual
   graph execution, flash-attention path, full GPU offload and model support.
   Keep the pinned authority unchanged. If evidence identifies a materially
   stronger configuration or the inspected newer local llama revision, admit
   at most two supplementary configurations, frozen before timing, and measure
   them under the same quality/resource contract. Authenticate each separately;
   failure to build/run a supplementary baseline limits this conclusion but
   does not invalidate the pinned comparison.
4. Where supported, compare equivalent packed KV or weight precision policies
   on llama as a separately labeled quality-matched baseline. If an encoding
   cannot be matched, report the asymmetry; do not call a changed-quantization
   Quartz win an identical-arithmetic kernel win. Do not introduce an unrelated
   model/download or spend an unbounded tuning budget finding a stronger llama.
5. Build a mechanism disposition table across OPT-117–122: bytes/time upper
   bound, implemented candidates, quality/state blockers, complete-request
   outcome and remaining uncertainty. Distinguish measured losses from missing
   evidence and capability failures. Review whether rejected ideas changed
   meaningfully under the final stack; reopen only with a new causal reason,
   not another random launch/tile choice.
6. State one supported conclusion per workload: demonstrated Quartz advantage;
   measurable headroom with a specific remaining mechanism; no demonstrated
   worthwhile headroom within the tested scope; or insufficient evidence.
   A practical stopping conclusion requires a complete traffic account within
   measurement uncertainty and plausible remaining scoped savings below the
   predeclared materiality threshold (default 2% request time). If unsupported,
   say what measurement is missing rather than proclaiming llama optimal.
7. Discuss cross-token weight amortization only to explain the scope of the
   bound: ordinary autoregressive batch-one execution must respect sequential
   token dependencies. Speculation/MTP, batching/concurrent sessions, model
   replacement/retraining and sparse attention are outside this approved
   batch. Their exclusion prevents universal claims; it does not authorize
   implementation here.
