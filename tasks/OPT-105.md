## Outcome

Test one new prompt-MMQ schedule: a 64x128 output/prompt tile with two raw-Q4 X
stages and the retained async-Y pipeline. Keep it only if complete FFN and
P4096 improve; otherwise retain the selected 128x128 single-X-stage schedule.

## Rationale

P4096 needs about 52.9 ms/prompt for parity and 114.4 ms for the unchanged +5%
bar. Q4 FFN remains the largest prompt component. OPT-065 found 64x128 slower
than 128x128 with async Y only. OPT-066 then showed async raw-X was valuable,
but 128x128 can fit only one raw-X stage under the 101,376-byte opt-in shared
limit. The smaller 64x128 tile can fit two raw-X stages and may overlap the
next unpack/load rather than waiting on the single slot. OPT-097's split wait
on the 128x128 one-slot layout remains rejected.

## Implementation

1. Keep the exact current control `q4_i128_j128_fma_async_x` with joined waits.
   Add exactly one candidate tile/schedule `q4_i64_j128_fma_async_x2`: existing
   two-stage Y, two alternating raw-X slots, unchanged decoded-X/MMA fragments,
   FMA association and epilogue.
2. Prove the complete async-group lifetime. Prefetch X[k+1] into the free slot
   while computing k; wait before unpack, never reuse a live slot, drain the
   last stage, and use the synchronous reference for tails/misalignment. Do not
   reuse OPT-097's invalidated scheduling assumption.
3. Record exact shared bytes, registers, spills, occupancy, cp.async/SASS,
   barrier stalls and eligible/fallback launch ids. Test K256/512/5120/17408,
   stale-address relaunch, graph/eager and M/N/K tails.
4. Same-math aligned outputs must be bitwise identical to the control. Preserve
   `shared_y_swiglu_q8`, down-leg quantization, Q4 association and every decode
   selector.
5. Screen one full production FFN and then 64 rotating FFNs. Apply the shared
   3+10 component protocol and five-pair P4096 gate; D128/D2048 are gross guards.

## Acceptance

- Async ownership and fallback are exact; bitwise output identity passes.
